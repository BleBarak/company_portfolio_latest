Teaching our robot not to give up

  • Robo City Tech Team
  • 8 Nov 2026
  • 5 min read

One of the jobs we want our robot to do is check on the house while nobody is home. It drives from room to room and shows you what it sees on your phone. Homes are messy, though. A bag on the floor, a chair pushed out, a door half closed. Our first versions did the polite thing when the path was blocked: they stopped and reported that they had given up. That is useless if you are at work and want to know whether you left the stove on.

How the robot sees

The robot drives on hoverboard wheels and motors. It carries a Raspberry Pi, an Intel RealSense depth camera that sees in 3D in front of it, and a 360° LiDAR that measures the distance to the walls around it. From these it builds a map of the room and plans a path to where you tapped on your phone.

A ladder of things to try

Instead of one plan and a "give up" state, every self-driving mode now has a recovery ladder. When the way is blocked, the robot works through it step by step:

  • Wait a moment, in case a person or pet moves out of the way
  • Back up a little to make room
  • Turn whichever way is free. If one side is blocked it goes the other way round
  • Plan a new path around the obstacle and try again
  • Rest and retry instead of quitting for good

Only real safety reasons stop it for good: the emergency stop, a tilt, or losing the link to someone driving it by hand.

Turning in tight spaces

One lesson from testing: our robot refused to turn when it was close to a box, because it checked for a free circle all the way around itself. Now it only checks the area its body actually sweeps through during the next part of the turn. If that is blocked, it turns the other way round instead.

When the robot is searching for a person it has lost, it no longer just spins in one direction. It sweeps both ways, like we would.

It also works with your home

Because the robot is part of Purple Hub, it can use the rest of your smart home. When it drives through a dark room, it switches the lights on so its camera can see, and puts them back as they were afterwards.

We are still testing and tuning on the real robot, and we will share more as it improves.
